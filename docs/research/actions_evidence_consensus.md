# Context: We are designing "Kajo", a phone companion for people who have just fin

**Question:** Context: We are designing "Kajo", a phone companion for people who have just finished a course of rTMS for major depression (often treatment-resistant), to help them stay well at home. It passively senses sleep regularity, heart-rate variability and activity (smart ring), plus movement, daily rhythm and night-time phone use (phone). It learns each person's own baseline, detects sustained drift, and responds with ONE small, pre-chosen action at the right moment, then learns per person which action works. The candidate actions are: (a) a walk in nature – forest or seaside (and VR nature when going out is hard); (b) a music listening session; (c) a light session (bright light / dawn-simulation / light at the right time of day); (d) a short therapeutic or mood game (e.g. digital therapeutic games, cognitive training); (e) a nudge to message or meet a named friend (social contact); also consider brief behavioural activation, brief mindfulness, and physical exercise micro-bouts.

Please produce a deep, structured literature review answering:
1. For EACH candidate action, what is the best evidence (meta-analyses, Cochrane reviews, RCTs) that it reduces depressive symptoms in adults with depression, with effect sizes (SMD/Hedges g, response/remission), certainty, and whether evidence exists specifically for (i) relapse prevention / maintenance after remission or response, (ii) brief, self-delivered, at-home or smartphone-delivered versions, (iii) acute short-term mood effects (minutes to hours) that a just-in-time system could exploit. Flag which actions have essentially no maintenance evidence.
2. Evidence on just-in-time adaptive interventions (JITAIs) and micro-randomized trials for depression or mood: do they work, what proximal outcomes were used, engagement/adherence, and burden.
3. Methods for learning per-person "what works": n-of-1 trials, contextual bandits / reinforcement learning in mobile health (e.g. HeartSteps and similar), personalised recommendation of behavioural interventions in depression — what has been shown, sample sizes, outcomes, pitfalls (exploration vs safety, sparse rewards, confounding by regression to the mean).
4. Evidence that passive-sensing-triggered interventions (closed-loop: detect drift, then intervene) improve outcomes versus monitoring alone — including null results (e.g. MONARCA trials in bipolar disorder).
5. Engagement and ease of use for people recovering from depression: what predicts sustained use of mental-health apps, dropout rates, effect of minimal-burden/passive designs, and design features that keep people engaged over months.
6. Any evidence on combining TMS with home behavioural/digital interventions afterwards (post-TMS continuation with apps, exercise, psychotherapy, light) to prolong response.

Output: a structured report with a summary evidence table (action | best evidence | effect size | certainty | maintenance evidence? | home/brief evidence? | acute-effect evidence?), then sections for questions 2–6, a list of key gaps, and practical design implications. Prefer meta-analyses and RCTs from top journals; clearly label small or pilot studies; give years. Be explicit where evidence is absent.

# Evidence for **Kajo’s Post-rTMS Companion**

Kajo’s candidate actions have evidence for treating depression, but evidence for **brief, sensing-triggered actions that prevent relapse after rTMS is largely absent**. The central distinction is between an effective treatment programme, an immediate mood benefit, and demonstrated prevention of another depressive episode.

**Reading the estimates:** negative SMD/Hedges’ *g* generally favours treatment; some reviews report positive values for benefit. Certainty below refers to the studied intervention—not automatically to Kajo’s smaller version. Where no formal rating is supplied, the assessment is qualitative.

### Nature and Music

Nature-walking reviews support mood and rumination benefits, but clinical-depression studies remain scarce and methodologically heterogeneous [[20.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/ma-lin-effectiveness-of-naturebased-walking-interventions/w:f0a7cf58-e166-4411-8001-a20e0deaaaf1/) [[3.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/balanzá‐martínez-cerveramartínez-lifestyle-prescription-for-depression-with-a-focus/w:1ad1626a-7777-42e0-b201-ddfd085cf108/) [[21.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/grassini-a-systematic-review-and-metaanalysis-of-nature/w:5b825d20-27e6-45de-bb0d-fe3ae7c98634/). Forest reviews similarly identify weak methods, high heterogeneity, publication bias, and inadequate long-term follow-up [[22.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/lee-choi-effects-of-forest-therapy-on-depressive-symptoms/w:60ca53c6-0836-42c6-ae7a-2647b2ad6d13/) [[23.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/lu-tang-effects-of-forest-therapy-on-depressive-symptoms-a/w:99f2a093-3408-45e3-9ab1-a630b06d8f7a/). The 37-person MDD walking trial found no group-by-time interaction, limiting claims about when or how long benefit lasted; it tested **60 minutes**, not a five-minute walk [[2.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/watkins‐martin-bolanis-the-effects-of-walking-in-nature-on-negative-and/w:4852ccfc-b259-460a-b668-d043b220c48c/) [[2.4]](https://consensus.app/papers/watkins‐martin-bolanis-the-effects-of-walking-in-nature-on-negative-and/w:4852ccfc-b259-460a-b668-d043b220c48c/). Seaside-specific superiority and relapse prevention are not established in this supplied corpus.

Music evidence spans listening, active participation, and therapist-supported emotional processing rather than one uniform intervention [[24.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/leubner-hinterberger-reviewing-the-effectiveness-of-music-interventions/w:282a0616-924c-4897-9132-db035c2eff31/). Tang et al. (2020) reported SMD −0.66 for music therapy and −1.33 for music medicine, but included diverse clinical and nonclinical populations [[18.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/tang-huang-effects-of-music-therapy-on-depression-a/w:395ee064-e071-4c18-a74f-3c2bc3b7a5f6/) [[18.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/tang-huang-effects-of-music-therapy-on-depression-a/w:395ee064-e071-4c18-a74f-3c2bc3b7a5f6/). Post-treatment effects were nonsignificant at one and three months in the limited follow-up evidence [[18.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/tang-huang-effects-of-music-therapy-on-depression-a/w:395ee064-e071-4c18-a74f-3c2bc3b7a5f6/).

Repeated listening over more than three weeks has supportive evidence, while older-adult findings favour preferred listening exceeding 60 minutes weekly—not necessarily tiny sessions [[25.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/chan-wong-the-effectiveness-of-music-listening-in-reducing/w:8095cf37-92d0-417b-81b0-608e85c58794/) [[26.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/dhippayom-saensook-comparative-effects-of-music-interventions-on/w:6a40b81b-39ec-4497-9352-8063321d3d8f/). A 20-person MDD pilot combined music with vibrotactile stimulation, preventing attribution to ordinary listening alone [[27.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/janzen-shirawi-a-pilot-study-investigating-the-effect-of/w:4058baf6-ba92-40d7-8f95-0ae3c5d5458c/) [[27.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/janzen-shirawi-a-pilot-study-investigating-the-effect-of/w:4058baf6-ba92-40d7-8f95-0ae3c5d5458c/). Well-being improvements are not equivalent to depression remission, and qualitative reports include distressing memories and exhaustion [[28.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/zhang-lu-impact-of-musicbased-interventions-on-subjective/w:67e0f661-c160-4e17-9d1d-27e08efe4bac/) [[29.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/snape-saarikallio-depressed-adults’-experiences-of-listening-to/w:a7f826b6-f461-418b-b08a-9f2526fb48d8/).

### Light, Games, and Contact

Bright-light response/remission benefits occur both before and after four weeks, not as demonstrated minute-scale rescue effects [[6.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/almeida-moraes-bright-light-therapy-for-nonseasonal-depressive/w:52916092-6408-4b55-a2a1-0d28cc763538/). TRD-specific reports exist, but optimal predictors and timing remain unsettled [[30.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/rajačić-šagud-illuminating-the-way-the-role-of-bright-light/w:e0f7d095-8dcc-45a4-a2eb-40eeafb1e3e6/) [[30.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/rajačić-šagud-illuminating-the-way-the-role-of-bright-light/w:e0f7d095-8dcc-45a4-a2eb-40eeafb1e3e6/). Bipolar recommendations emphasise monitoring and antimanic safeguards; they explicitly identify maintenance evidence as unresolved [[31.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/geoffroy-palagini-light-therapy-for-bipolar-disorders-clinical/w:682de826-b7db-4c1f-ab52-eda86ae7f4f8/) [[31.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/geoffroy-palagini-light-therapy-for-bipolar-disorders-clinical/w:682de826-b7db-4c1f-ab52-eda86ae7f4f8/). Dawn simulation should not inherit bright-light effect estimates without its own evidence.

CCT has a notable exception to the maintenance gap: **92 remitted patients**, randomised against active control, showed lower one-year recurrence, OR 0.38, *p*=.04, but no corresponding long-term self-reported functioning advantage [[32.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/hoorelbeke-bergh-preventing-recurrence-of-depression-longterm/w:c3c586b3-e75d-4a06-af78-4e750f079700/) [[32.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/hoorelbeke-bergh-preventing-recurrence-of-depression-longterm/w:c3c586b3-e75d-4a06-af78-4e750f079700/) [[32.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/hoorelbeke-bergh-preventing-recurrence-of-depression-longterm/w:c3c586b3-e75d-4a06-af78-4e750f079700/). A separate multicentre study found self-reported but not clinician-rated depression superiority at three months [[33.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/baglioni-battelino-effectiveness-of-cognitive-remediation-in-subjects/w:2c66cd57-006d-4989-920f-04a8350b28d9/) [[33.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/baglioni-battelino-effectiveness-of-cognitive-remediation-in-subjects/w:2c66cd57-006d-4989-920f-04a8350b28d9/). Exergames did not outperform conventional exercise; cognitive-training apps also produced smaller depression effects than mental-health-focused apps [[9.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/abdalrazaq-aljafar-the-effectiveness-of-serious-games-for-alleviating/w:3abc3405-1abd-4e1a-88a6-7f9c97f6da90/) [[34.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/firth-torous-the-efficacy-of-smartphone‐based-mental-health/w:639e0c01-238f-47a3-bd86-f0a6756a0815/).

Social-contact evidence is mixed: a 2025 older-adult meta-analysis found nonsignificant pooled effects, and MDD lifestyle guidelines based social-support recommendations on expert opinion [[35.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/faronbi-eromosele-effects-of-social-support-interventions-on/w:52af6d22-d2dc-42d2-9a60-1a3ee82d4306/) [[36.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/marx-manger-clinical-guidelines-for-the-use-of-lifestylebased/w:55c4fc07-15c2-451c-b7e9-e80a6a66c3c8/). Structured befriending therefore supports plausibility, not proof that prompting one message prevents relapse.

### Activation, Mindfulness, and Exercise

BA has comparable short-term efficacy to CBT; a 2026 synthesis estimated self-guided BA SMD 0.36, versus 0.67 for adult outpatient BA overall [[11.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/uphoff-ekers-behavioural-activation-therapy-for-depression-in/w:70a27083-c4e8-4d94-bdb2-e80f3bad2562/) [[37.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/cuijpers-čihařová-behavioral-activation-for-depression-a/w:98197508-c5b2-4d37-a3b5-27e230b1fb7d/) [[37.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/cuijpers-čihařová-behavioral-activation-for-depression-a/w:98197508-c5b2-4d37-a3b5-27e230b1fb7d/). However, internet BA showed no significant six-month advantage, and prior therapist-led BA’s advantage over medication withdrawal was not statistically significant in the maintenance trial [[12.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/alber-krämer-internetbased-behavioral-activation-for-depression/w:af88ffa4-68c4-4878-86aa-fb81b20bb544/) [[19.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/dobson-hollon-randomized-trial-of-behavioral-activation/w:cf53017d-7ae6-4b3d-8e83-14565024db29/).

MBCT relapse evidence is stronger than evidence for isolated mindfulness: foundational findings favoured people with at least three previous episodes, while PREVENT found no superiority over maintenance antidepressants [[38.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/teasdale-segal-prevention-of-relapserecurrence-in-major/w:e1c6be26-9962-4cb4-8285-26795ea909b2/) [[39.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/kuyken-hayes-effectiveness-and-costeffectiveness-of/w:42566393-36a3-4a78-b52c-f6d650bf763d/). Broader psychological-intervention IPD analyses report relapse HR 0.60 overall and in partial remitters, supporting access to preventive therapy rather than substituting micro-actions for it [[40.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/breedvelt-karyotaki-an-individual-participant-data-metaanalysis-of/w:eb883b8b-50b4-4185-9463-c895542cece2/) [[41.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/gülpen-breedvelt-psychological-interventions-for-preventing-relapse/w:00b2cd2f-d536-42e1-82f6-c0eb31f33c7f/).

Exercise estimates shrink under stricter methods: Cochrane 2026 found SMD −0.46 in seven stronger trials and very uncertain long-term effects [[16.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/clegg-hill-exercise-for-depression/w:4cbe626a-a086-4c24-8e38-36354952a687/) [[16.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/clegg-hill-exercise-for-depression/w:4cbe626a-a086-4c24-8e38-36354952a687/). Other syntheses support benefit but differ with comparators and eligibility, including one nonsignificant restricted analysis [[42.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/heißel-heinen-exercise-as-medicine-for-depressive-symptoms-a/w:e2da18d7-7dc3-43f8-a309-8690fdeac4f1/) [[43.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/morres-hatzigeorgiadis-aerobic-exercise-for-adult-patients-with-major/w:521b1c44-d6fb-4bca-8fd9-00b226d80972/) [[44.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/bedoya-lópez-physical-exercise-and-major-depressive-disorder-in/w:e15805a2-11f5-4a4a-aac6-e4711a6d8a46/). Acute exercise’s pooled affect estimate is **pre–post**, with a prediction interval crossing zero—not proof of controlled micro-bout efficacy [[17.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/bourke-patten-the-acute-affective-response-to-physical-activity/w:ef0f56cf-68ec-431d-b5d6-420de0a94ad1/) [[17.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/bourke-patten-the-acute-affective-response-to-physical-activity/w:ef0f56cf-68ec-431d-b5d6-420de0a94ad1/).

## 2. JITAIs and Micro-Randomisation

Depression-specific JITAI evidence remains preliminary: a 2025 review found only five mental-health JITAIs, with little passive sensing and poorly substantiated decision rules [[45.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/genugten-thong-beyond-the-current-state-of-justintime-adaptive/w:2e808653-6a6f-4ff2-8041-a97d78164491/) [[45.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/genugten-thong-beyond-the-current-state-of-justintime-adaptive/w:2e808653-6a6f-4ff2-8041-a97d78164491/) [[45.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/genugten-thong-beyond-the-current-state-of-justintime-adaptive/w:2e808653-6a6f-4ff2-8041-a97d78164491/). Its MDD rumination pilot had only 18 participants despite large reported effects on episode count and duration [[45.4]](https://consensus.app/papers/genugten-thong-beyond-the-current-state-of-justintime-adaptive/w:2e808653-6a6f-4ff2-8041-a97d78164491/) [[45.5]](https://consensus.app/papers/genugten-thong-beyond-the-current-state-of-justintime-adaptive/w:2e808653-6a6f-4ff2-8041-a97d78164491/). Older cross-domain JITAI effect sizes should not be treated as depression-specific efficacy [[46.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/wang-miller-justinthemoment-adaptive-interventions-jitai-a/w:a0e8df06-52b9-4087-945f-262c865974d1/).

MRTs estimate **whether, when, and under what circumstances** a prompt changes a proximal outcome [[47.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/qian-walton-the-microrandomized-trial-for-developing-digital/w:56425f32-4644-403a-86be-ddfbed1a819e/). HeartSteps used next-30-minute steps: walking suggestions increased these by 24%, but effects declined over time [[48.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/klasnja-smith-efficacy-of-contextually-tailored-suggestions-for/w:219b9e03-4c9d-48c0-851b-047e1c6b7204/) [[48.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/klasnja-smith-efficacy-of-contextually-tailored-suggestions-for/w:219b9e03-4c9d-48c0-851b-047e1c6b7204/) [[48.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/klasnja-smith-efficacy-of-contextually-tailored-suggestions-for/w:219b9e03-4c9d-48c0-851b-047e1c6b7204/). JADE proposes momentary activation/well-being and PHQ-9 as proximal/distal outcomes, but remains a protocol, not efficacy evidence [[49.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/chakhssi-nuij-just-in-time-adaptive-interventions-for-behavioral/w:00463fb7-b235-4296-b8cb-788b1bb63f00/) [[49.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/chakhssi-nuij-just-in-time-adaptive-interventions-for-behavioral/w:00463fb7-b235-4296-b8cb-788b1bb63f00/).

Kajo should distinguish **predicting mood** from **estimating an action’s causal effect**. MRT randomisation and recorded assignment probabilities provide a defensible route to causal learning, unlike interpreting improvement after a drift-triggered action as proof it worked [[47.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/qian-walton-the-microrandomized-trial-for-developing-digital/w:56425f32-4644-403a-86be-ddfbed1a819e/) [[47.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/qian-walton-the-microrandomized-trial-for-developing-digital/w:56425f32-4644-403a-86be-ddfbed1a819e/).

**Proposed design:** restrict exploration to pre-approved actions, include “no prompt,” and assess completion, immediate mood, next-day functioning, and eventual relapse separately. These are design implications of JITAI frameworks that explicitly include no intervention and distinguish proximal from distal outcomes [[53.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/nahumshani-smith-justintime-adaptive-interventions-jitais-in-mobile/w:fef1ff1f-523d-4f11-b020-be3430489f6d/) [[49.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/chakhssi-nuij-just-in-time-adaptive-interventions-for-behavioral/w:00463fb7-b235-4296-b8cb-788b1bb63f00/). Sparse observations, carryover, and regression to the mean make eight-way individual optimisation ambitious; pooled learning with cautious individual updating requires prospective validation.

## 4. Sensing-Triggered Closed Loops

Prediction is better established than clinical benefit. Longitudinal depression evidence comprised only nine studies; personalised/multimodal models performed better, but individual clinical decision support remained insufficiently validated [[54.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/amin-schreynemackers-use-of-mobile-sensing-data-for-longitudinal/w:7e6d4f84-2bef-4e49-b8f8-f2faaadbf9e3/) [[54.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/amin-schreynemackers-use-of-mobile-sensing-data-for-longitudinal/w:7e6d4f84-2bef-4e49-b8f8-f2faaadbf9e3/) [[54.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/amin-schreynemackers-use-of-mobile-sensing-data-for-longitudinal/w:7e6d4f84-2bef-4e49-b8f8-f2faaadbf9e3/). Broader reviews found scarce external validation and inconsistent missing-data handling [[55.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/shen-qi-passive-sensing-for-mental-health-monitoring-using/w:e1a9d4e6-02d2-4278-af0e-9f8d060c5030/) [[56.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/angel-lewis-digital-health-tools-for-the-passive-monitoring-of/w:8ee80188-1501-4e6c-8fef-0dcc6ddba6e2/). Sleep stability, mobility, and homestay show associations, not established causal intervention targets [[56.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/angel-lewis-digital-health-tools-for-the-passive-monitoring-of/w:8ee80188-1501-4e6c-8fef-0dcc6ddba6e2/) [[56.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/angel-lewis-digital-health-tools-for-the-passive-monitoring-of/w:8ee80188-1501-4e6c-8fef-0dcc6ddba6e2/).

Counterevidence matters: personalised control limits failed to detect bipolar episodes adequately, while MONARCA I showed no symptom benefit and a tendency toward sustained depression [[57.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/ludwig-bittendorf-predicting-depressive-and-manic-episodes-in/w:f5ca0a56-c64c-47e4-a250-7fc5e54747c5/) [[57.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/ludwig-bittendorf-predicting-depressive-and-manic-episodes-in/w:f5ca0a56-c64c-47e4-a250-7fc5e54747c5/) [[58.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/faurholt‐jepsen-frost-daily-electronic-selfmonitoring-in-bipolar/w:686d3dda-7eb1-4d02-ab5a-49eb0d30bfda/) [[58.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/faurholt‐jepsen-frost-daily-electronic-selfmonitoring-in-bipolar/w:686d3dda-7eb1-4d02-ab5a-49eb0d30bfda/). RADMIS found no readmission or depression benefit; SmartBipolar likewise found no advantage from adding treatment/clinical feedback [[59.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/faurholt‐jepsen-tønning-reducing-the-rate-of-psychiatric-re‐admissions-in/w:892bbc6f-c484-47ae-a45f-b754d755112f/) [[59.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/faurholt‐jepsen-tønning-reducing-the-rate-of-psychiatric-re‐admissions-in/w:892bbc6f-c484-47ae-a45f-b754d755112f/) [[60.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/faurholt‐jepsen-risom-the-effect-of-smartphonebased-monitoring-and/w:1386a9bd-c2a5-46c1-86f2-9c14593f3088/) [[60.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/faurholt‐jepsen-risom-the-effect-of-smartphonebased-monitoring-and/w:1386a9bd-c2a5-46c1-86f2-9c14593f3088/).

Positive precedents remain preliminary: MOSS improvements rested on 12 adherent symptomatic participants without randomised confirmation; Vira homestay changes predicted improvement in a 47-person proof-of-concept [[61.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/wahle-kowatsch-mobile-sensing-and-support-for-people-with/w:8c9e8865-8bbf-4b90-91cf-7ba0e2b8e431/) [[61.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/wahle-kowatsch-mobile-sensing-and-support-for-people-with/w:8c9e8865-8bbf-4b90-91cf-7ba0e2b8e431/) [[62.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/funkhouser-weiner-early-changes-in-passively-sensed-homestay-predict/w:cbf37a44-9f57-4437-ae68-38dde6a3bdfe/) [[62.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/funkhouser-weiner-early-changes-in-passively-sensed-homestay-predict/w:cbf37a44-9f57-4437-ae68-38dde6a3bdfe/). One reviewed rhythm-feedback study reported fewer/shorter episodes, but this isolated finding does not establish Kajo’s closed-loop efficacy [[54.4]](https://consensus.app/papers/amin-schreynemackers-use-of-mobile-sensing-data-for-longitudinal/w:7e6d4f84-2bef-4e49-b8f8-f2faaadbf9e3/).

## 5. Engagement and Burden

Across 79 app trials, uptake was 92.4%, adherence 61.8%, post-test attrition 18.6%, and follow-up attrition 28.4%, with wide prediction intervals [[63.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/liu-torous-uptake-adherence-and-attrition-in-clinical-trials/w:35113975-c49b-4f42-bcc5-7e3f1154985e/) [[63.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/liu-torous-uptake-adherence-and-attrition-in-clinical-trials/w:35113975-c49b-4f42-bcc5-7e3f1154985e/) [[63.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/liu-torous-uptake-adherence-and-attrition-in-clinical-trials/w:35113975-c49b-4f42-bcc5-7e3f1154985e/) [[63.4]](https://consensus.app/papers/liu-torous-uptake-adherence-and-attrition-in-clinical-trials/w:35113975-c49b-4f42-bcc5-7e3f1154985e/). Human contact, reminders, and absence of gamification were associated with lower attrition, but another monitoring review linked reminders to worse adherence and noted confounding [[63.5]](https://consensus.app/papers/liu-torous-uptake-adherence-and-attrition-in-clinical-trials/w:35113975-c49b-4f42-bcc5-7e3f1154985e/) [[64.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/wright-roe-dropout-attrition-adherence-and-compliance-in-mood/w:d48e5ba6-688f-494b-866c-e8a300e56fec/). Passive assessment did **not** demonstrate superior retention or adherence over active assessment [[64.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/wright-roe-dropout-attrition-adherence-and-compliance-in-mood/w:d48e5ba6-688f-494b-866c-e8a300e56fec/).

Apps produce small average symptom effects—*g*=0.28 in a large synthesis—and active-control comparisons yield smaller effects than inactive controls [[65.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/linardon-cuijpers-the-efficacy-of-app‐supported-smartphone/w:33db8f8a-6525-48fa-b151-4e894d4c54f4/) [[34.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/firth-torous-the-efficacy-of-smartphone‐based-mental-health/w:639e0c01-238f-47a3-bd86-f0a6756a0815/). Coaching deserves consideration: Vira’s coach-guided version improved primary symptoms while its self-guided version did not [[62.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/funkhouser-weiner-early-changes-in-passively-sensed-homestay-predict/w:cbf37a44-9f57-4437-ae68-38dde6a3bdfe/). Exercise dropout averaged 18.1%, with greater baseline depression predicting dropout and specialist supervision predicting retention [[66.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/stubbs-vancampfort-dropout-from-exercise-randomized-controlled-trials/w:a1633ace-d649-43ca-a0b0-7d8525451f79/) [[66.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/stubbs-vancampfort-dropout-from-exercise-randomized-controlled-trials/w:a1633ace-d649-43ca-a0b0-7d8525451f79/).

For Kajo, “one small action” is a reasonable **burden hypothesis**, not a proven retention strategy. Co-design should avoid patronising feedback and permit selective reflection, pausing, and user-chosen involvement of services [[67.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/wright-rawsthorne-recommendations-for-research-and-clinical/w:c401e5d1-9008-4e75-aec1-8380a0fd1b72/) [[67.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/wright-rawsthorne-recommendations-for-research-and-clinical/w:c401e5d1-9008-4e75-aec1-8380a0fd1b72/).

## 6. Continuation After rTMS

Among initial rTMS responders, pooled sustained response was 66.5% at three months, 52.9% at six months, and 46.3% at twelve months; these are not untreated natural-history relapse rates [[68.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/senova-cotovio-durability-of-antidepressant-response-to/w:0f08a658-0949-4837-8cdf-a788794d9fd0/). Maintenance rTMS evidence is heterogeneous, with disagreement over adequate schedules and no universal regimen [[69.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/d’andrea-mancusi-investigating-the-role-of-maintenance-tms/w:c37f884a-b49e-4632-a3ce-e6460130fb10/) [[70.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/chen-chou-trajectory-of-changes-in-depressive-symptoms-after/w:70b76168-0298-4bf8-8092-b3b8e10abc41/) [[71.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/dalhuisen-steenkamp-consensus-statement-on-the-maintenance-treatment/w:326d9d94-e467-445d-b968-4a7a6dc57803/).

**Direct evidence for Kajo’s proposed post-rTMS behavioural continuation is absent or preliminary.** Preventive cognitive therapy after rTMS has a feasibility protocol, not efficacy results [[72.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/lange-albers-comparing-the-feasibility-of-inperson-and-online/w:292ce452-6748-4b50-a79b-2981ac84113c/). A 36-person digital-coaching trial studied adjuncts **during** rTMS, with no difference between its randomised digital conditions [[73.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/rosenberg-barnes‐horowitz-does-mental-health-coaching-improve-efficacy-of/w:5833e650-9c7d-4269-96b7-c7558c4026cf/) [[73.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/rosenberg-barnes‐horowitz-does-mental-health-coaching-improve-efficacy-of/w:5833e650-9c7d-4269-96b7-c7558c4026cf/). Concurrent psychotherapy findings are promising but naturalistic; newer combined-CBT meta-analysis benefits concern symptom treatment, not post-course relapse prevention [[74.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/donse-padberg-simultaneous-rtms-and-psychotherapy-in-major/w:b50cd6a0-3389-42f8-99f6-6876ebea8e99/) [[74.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/donse-padberg-simultaneous-rtms-and-psychotherapy-in-major/w:b50cd6a0-3389-42f8-99f6-6876ebea8e99/) [[75.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/liu-wang-efficacy-of-repetitive-transcranial-magnetic/w:84a0c2c0-2a2a-463f-8a85-15f48e0032bb/). Another adjunct review found no superiority over active rTMS alone [[76.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/giron-tang-antidepressant-efficacy-of-administering/w:e7159715-7f70-4402-a309-8f6ce0d06aa2/).

Even maintenance-stimulation findings require population checks: the large clustered-rTMS trial enrolled **medication remitters**, whereas the directly post-rTMS sham-controlled trial randomised only 17 patients [[77.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/wang-wang-clustered-repetitive-transcranial-magnetic/w:c08a9551-0885-44bc-9e9a-e91fc68cd521/) [[78.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/benadhira-thomas-a-randomized-shamcontrolled-study-of-maintenance/w:2c564059-2f22-452d-bc7d-f286cb7c8495/).

The largest gap is **post-rTMS prevention using brief actions**, rather than full treatment programmes. Nature/music follow-up is limited, while preventive psychological therapies and specialised CCT provide closer—but still indirect—maintenance precedents [[22.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/lee-choi-effects-of-forest-therapy-on-depressive-symptoms/w:60ca53c6-0836-42c6-ae7a-2647b2ad6d13/) [[18.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/tang-huang-effects-of-music-therapy-on-depression-a/w:395ee064-e071-4c18-a74f-3c2bc3b7a5f6/) [[40.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/breedvelt-karyotaki-an-individual-participant-data-metaanalysis-of/w:eb883b8b-50b4-4185-9463-c895542cece2/) [[32.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/hoorelbeke-bergh-preventing-recurrence-of-depression-longterm/w:c3c586b3-e75d-4a06-af78-4e750f079700/).

- **Prioritise meaningful activation and walking**, while treating the minimum effective micro-dose as an unanswered question [[37.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/cuijpers-čihařová-behavioral-activation-for-depression-a/w:98197508-c5b2-4d37-a3b5-27e230b1fb7d/) [[15.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/noetel-sanders-effect-of-exercise-for-depression-systematic/w:1faac512-9f1b-4d46-a2a4-48943a2a4c7c/).
- Keep **light clinician-configured and scheduled**, rather than allowing unrestricted experimental timing [[31.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/geoffroy-palagini-light-therapy-for-bipolar-disorders-clinical/w:682de826-b7db-4c1f-ab52-eda86ae7f4f8/) [[31.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/geoffroy-palagini-light-therapy-for-bipolar-disorders-clinical/w:682de826-b7db-4c1f-ab52-eda86ae7f4f8/).
- Retain occasional symptom/context confirmation because passive measures do not yet replace clinical outcomes [[67.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/wright-rawsthorne-recommendations-for-research-and-clinical/w:c401e5d1-9008-4e75-aec1-8380a0fd1b72/).
- Test adaptive prompting against both monitoring-only and nonadaptive prompting, using MRT optimisation before a relapse-endpoint trial [[47.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/qian-walton-the-microrandomized-trial-for-developing-digital/w:56425f32-4644-403a-86be-ddfbed1a819e/) [[50.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/aguilera-avalos-effectiveness-of-a-digital-health-intervention/w:13573c4b-d059-4abc-a03f-acbbe1143bee/).

**Bottom line:** Kajo is best positioned as an investigational, low-burden adjunct to an agreed continuation-care plan—not an established relapse-prevention treatment. Its strongest testable proposition is whether personalised prompts improve useful daily behaviours without burden, followed by whether those gains actually prolong post-rTMS wellness.
