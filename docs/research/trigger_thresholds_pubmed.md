# Hiillos trigger thresholds: what PubMed supports (14 Nov 2026)

**Searched in Sky's browser on PubMed, 14 Nov 2026.** Every figure below was read in the PubMed abstract only, not the full paper. Author names are given only where the abstract page showed them.

## Bottom line

**No study found here validates a fixed "activity down X% → prompt" cut-off for depression.** The method with the best support is a different one. Learn each person's own baseline. Then flag when a smoothed trend (an EWMA control chart) drifts beyond that person's own control limit. Two Dutch studies used this to catch depression recurrence weeks to a month ahead. The absolute numbers that do exist (step bands, home-stay hours) are population anchors. Use them as a backup check, not as the trigger.

## Suggested Hiillos trigger rules — team design inference, NOT validated cut-offs

Every number in this section is a **starting setting to tune**. None comes from a study, except where a PMID is given.

1. **Baseline:** 14–28 days of data after TMS, per person. For each signal, store the person's own mean and day-to-day variation: daily steps, step variability, time at home, sleep midpoint, sleep time and its variation.
2. **Detection:** run a smoothed trend chart (EWMA) per signal against that person's own control limits, as in PMIDs 36808958 and 35833374. In standard process-control practice the smoothing weight is about 0.2 and the limit is about 3 standard deviations. Those values come from general statistical practice and are not checked against these papers. Require the drift to last several days (e.g. 3 of 5) before acting.
3. **"Walk in the forest" prompt:** fire when the steps trend crosses the lower limit or step variability rises (PMID 40175826), **or** time at home rises above the personal limit on weekdays (PMID 35089148). As a backup population check, also fire if steps stay **under 5,000/day** for several days (PMID 39680407). A gain of **+1,000 steps/day** is a realistic small goal (PMID 34979178). A "% drop" can be shown to the user as a readable label, e.g. "30% below your usual". The 30% itself is an arbitrary display number, not evidence.
4. **Sleep-regularity nudge:** fire when night-to-night variation in sleep time or sleep midpoint rises above the personal limit. Respond with an evening routine and morning daylight prompt (PMIDs 39038618, 33603132).
5. **Check-in content:** add **one restlessness/irritability item and one worry/negative-thoughts item**. These were the strongest early signs (PMIDs 35833374, 36808958). Caveat: those studies used 5 check-ins a day, and the signal weakened with fewer.
6. **Escalation:** sensors alone never alert a clinician. Sensor drift triggers an extra PHQ-9. The PHQ-9 drives the clinician list (see the symptoms-and-signals report). Any answer above zero on PHQ-9 item 9 goes straight to a human.
7. **Context guards:** suppress or annotate triggers during illness, travel, weekends (time at home behaves differently), seasonal daylight in Finland, and medication changes (these change HRV).

## Gaps

- No post-TMS study was found.
- No validated %-drop threshold was found.
- No trial was found showing that a sensor-triggered walk prompt prevents relapse.
- The smart-ring step-variability result comes from bipolar disorder, not unipolar depression.
- Full texts were not read; the EWMA settings the studies used should be checked in the full papers.
