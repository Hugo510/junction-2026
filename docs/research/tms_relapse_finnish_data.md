# Catch relapse early after TMS ends

**Bottom line:** The best-supported product for the "Beyond the Clinic" challenge is not a home treatment device. It is a **structured early-warning loop**. It tracks symptoms at home after a TMS course, picks up worsening early, and sends the person back for a short TMS "rescue" or other proven care. The evidence for this is fairly clear. **Only about half of TMS responders are still responding at 6 months, and about 46% at 12 months** ([Senova et al. 2019](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:0f08a658-0949-4837-8cdf-a788794d9fd0/)). When early worsening was caught in one multisite study, **84% of patients regained benefit after TMS was reintroduced** ([Janicak et al. 2010](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/durability-of-clinical-benefit-with-transcranial-magnetic-stimula/)). Symptom-triggered "rescue" maintenance looks at least as good as fixed schedules, although the difference was not statistically significant ([Akram et al. 2024/2025](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:35db24da-5fca-413b-997a-be39e8c2c445/)). A 2026 Dutch-Flemish consensus statement records that patient representatives asked for **structured monitoring** to guide maintenance ([Dalhuisen et al. 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:326d9d94-e467-445d-b968-4a7a6dc57803/)). The HUS referral guideline, which covers the rTMS unit at Jorvi in Espoo, gives no booster protocol ([HUS 2025](https://www.hus.fi/en/node/6912)). Two warnings matter for the "smart" layer. Passive-sensing relapse models report AUCs of 0.70–0.88, but these come **mostly from internal validation** and 75% of the studies had high risk of bias ([Fang et al. 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:c96a12dd-6d0d-4377-9307-c2b12d13b32f/)). And the largest Nordic smartphone-monitoring trial found that **monitoring alone did not improve symptoms** ([MONARCA II](https://research.regionh.dk/en/publications/the-effect-of-smartphone-based-monitoring-on-illness-activity-in-/)). For data, the team can use Sotkanet, StatFin, Kela open data, THL reports and FinnGen summary statistics this weekend. All individual-level data need a Findata permit, with a **median processing time of 49 days** in 2025 ([Findata 2025](https://findata.fi/en/about-findata/annual-report-2025/)).

**How to read the flags in this report.** These are the research team's labels, carried over unchanged. **Verified** means the figure was read on the linked page. **[Secondary]** means it came from a news item, summary, lecture slide or abstract aggregator, so check the primary source before you put it on a slide. **[Verify]** means the source itself is uncertain or inconsistent. **[Unverified lead]** means it comes from model memory or a title only, so it is not evidence. Nothing marked unverified has been upgraded to fact. The researchers could not run live API calls to Sotkanet, StatFin or FinnGen from their sandbox, and PubMed often showed a captcha, so some records were read through mirrors.

## Half of TMS responders lose response within a year

### Acute TMS works, but most patients finish as partial responders

Standard left-DLPFC rTMS has sham-controlled support. In pragmatic, treatment-resistant samples, however, response and remission are modest. The best recent European trial, **BRIGhTMIND** (UK NHS, n=255), found **response of about 31–35% and remission of about 17–25%**, with little change from 8 to 26 weeks. Connectivity-guided targeting added no benefit over the standard site ([Morriss et al., Nat Med 2024](https://nottingham-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/33826256)). Theta-burst (iTBS) gives similar results in about 3 minutes of stimulation instead of about 37. The accelerated SNT/SAINT protocol showed a very large effect, but in a very small trial.

**Correction to the model-memory draft:** the draft said SAINT had "remission ~79% vs ~13%". The verified editorial reports **46.2% remission** at 4 weeks after treatment. The 79% figure is often quoted from the earlier open-label study and was not confirmed.

The practical result is that **most patients who finish a TMS course are partial responders or non-remitters**, which is the group at higher risk of relapse. Accelerated protocols reduce the number of clinic visits, but their durability is still unknown, so they make post-course monitoring *more* relevant. One more point matters for the loop design. Some patients respond late: in a small naturalistic study (n=31), **7 of 11 acute non-responders (63.6%) responded during 6 months of follow-up** ([Arici et al. 2020](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:d87d80dc-908a-4b72-8ce9-0a3048285ff9/)). A monitoring product therefore needs to track improvement as well as decline.

In absolute terms, out of about 100 responders, roughly 33 lose their response by 3 months and about 47 by 6 months. The Janicak data show that early worsening is common and usually reversible when it is caught. That is the clinical case for an early-warning loop. Relapse figures differ a lot between studies (from about 10% to about 54%), mainly because of different definitions (relapse vs loss of response) and populations (remitters vs responders). Remitters appear to relapse less than partial responders, so **depth of acute response is a plausible way to stratify risk**. This is an inference from the notes, not a tested predictor.

### Fixed-schedule maintenance is weakly evidenced; symptom-triggered rescue is the better fit

No consensus maintenance schedule exists. The only randomised-trial meta-analysis found (Wang et al. 2025: 9 RCTs, 837 participants, of which 3 were rTMS trials) gave an overall 6-month relapse risk ratio of **0.69 (0.45–1.04), which is not significant**. The rTMS-only result (RR 0.43) **rests on a single study**, and GRADE certainty was **very low to low** ([Wang et al., Alpha Psychiatry 2025, PMC12781222](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12781222)). A broader meta-analysis of 14 mostly non-randomised studies (N=705) found that maintenance was associated with improvement (SMD 0.75), with very high heterogeneity (I² 90%). Within it, **"rescue" maintenance (re-treating when worsening appears) had SMD −1.17, compared with −0.45 for fixed schedules (difference p = 0.11)** ([Akram et al. 2024/2025](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:35db24da-5fca-413b-997a-be39e8c2c445/)).

| Approach | What the evidence says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Tapering (e.g. 3×/week → weekly → monthly) | One RCT plus open-label studies; works best when symptom-guided. In two case reports, one patient relapsed on the monthly schedule | [Dalhuisen 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:326d9d94-e467-445d-b968-4a7a6dc57803/); [Joseph 2024](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:5c479e00-93de-4536-9c24-083d937ab1d7/) |
| Fixed maintenance | Single-session protocols give mixed results. Clustered protocols (about 5 sessions a month over several days) look best but have no controlled data. Two or fewer sessions a month appear ineffective | [Dalhuisen 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:326d9d94-e467-445d-b968-4a7a6dc57803/); [d'Andrea 2023](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:c37f884a-b49e-4632-a3ce-e6460130fb10/) |
| Retreatment on relapse / rescue | Effective in most previous responders, often with fewer sessions; common practice across 11 Dutch institutions | [Dalhuisen 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:326d9d94-e467-445d-b968-4a7a6dc57803/) |
| Finnish practice (2017) | No national consensus. Some hospitals stop at response; others continue every 2–4 weeks. In a 1-year pilot, about 57% (maintenance) vs 62% (follow-up only) needed more rTMS | [Lääkärilehti 2017](https://oys.fi/fincchta/wp-content/uploads/sites/21/2022/07/aivojen-magneettistimulaatio-masennuksen-hoidossa_ve_summary-2017.pdf) |
| Early reduced-frequency maintenance (retrospective, n=88) | PHQ-9 stable (5.27 → 5.58); longer, more frequent maintenance associated with better outcomes; no causal conclusion possible | [Poorganji 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:c52c0585-bfd0-4f78-af36-bde68606d5bd/) |
| Ongoing: Japanese multisite study (n=300 planned) | Weekly for 6 months, then biweekly; primary outcome is 12-month relapse. **Results not yet known, check** | [Yamazaki 2023](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:1b579568-0a08-427f-ac43-b0bb77caa74b/) |

The **Dutch-Flemish 2026 consensus** is the most product-relevant document in the whole evidence base. It sets out three approaches (tapering, maintenance, retreatment). It reports that retreatment is the common real-world choice, and it records that **patient representatives asked for early discussion, flexible scheduling and structured monitoring to guide maintenance** ([Dalhuisen et al. 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:326d9d94-e467-445d-b968-4a7a6dc57803/)). No verified head-to-head trial compares scheduled maintenance with symptom-triggered rescue, which is an open evidence gap.

### Finland has the hardware heritage but no post-TMS protocol

**Nexstim** announced FDA 510(k) clearance of its NBT navigated TMS system for depression on 13 November 2017 ([Nexstim](https://nexstim.com/news-and-events/news/press-release/nexstim-gains-fda-clearance-to-commercialise-its-nbtr-system-in-the-us-for-the-treatment-of-depressi)). A later clearance for a shorter protocol (likely K182700) was seen in search results but not read, so it is **[unverified]**. In the public sector, **HUS** routes rTMS referrals to neuromodulation units at Psychiatriakeskus (Helsinki), Peijas (Vantaa) and **Jorvi (Espoo)**. Eligibility is depression that persists after two adequate antidepressant trials, typically moderate to severe; **PHQ-9 ≥15 is given as an example**. Chronic depression lasting more than 2 years is not accepted at present. A course is typically **about 20 sessions at 3–5 days a week**, with no medication changes in the month before or during the course. HUS says the effect lasts months for most patients, and that intensifying medication or psychotherapy afterwards likely prolongs response, but **it gives no booster protocol** ([HUS guideline, updated 10.09.2025](https://www.hus.fi/en/node/6912)). In 2017, navigation-capable devices were reported in all university hospitals; a 10-session course was estimated at €2,000–3,000, and ECT was more cost-effective than rTMS ([Lääkärilehti 2017](https://oys.fi/fincchta/wp-content/uploads/sites/21/2022/07/aivojen-magneettistimulaatio-masennuksen-hoidossa_ve_summary-2017.pdf)). These 2017 figures **may have changed**. The researchers **did not find** Kela reimbursement rules for private rTMS, current private clinic prices, or any public TMS volume statistics.

Treatment-resistant depression (TRD), the TMS population, depends heavily on the definition used. The EMA defines it as lack of meaningful improvement after two antidepressants from different classes ([Paganin 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1733678/full)). In a Swedish register of 127,108 antidepressant starters with depression, **9.5% to 19.0%** met TRD criteria, depending on the definition ([Hägg et al., PLoS ONE 2020](https://doaj.org/article/788d3bcdd9404e5e82ca738f32057848)). The often-quoted "about 30% of patients have TRD" was **not verified**, and no Finnish TRD prevalence was found. Over the longer term, recurrence after a depressive episode in specialised care is about **60% at 5 years and 85% at 15 years**, against about 35% at 15 years in the general population ([Hardeveld et al. 2010, [Secondary]](https://scilit.com/publications/c5dc22e692d850ce41d935c25ed0c38a)).

Several gaps remain open. **Finland's own EHIS 2019 percentages were not retrieved**; pull them from Eurostat tables hlth_ehis_cd1e or hlth_ehis_mh1e. No newer IHME GBD, WHO Europe or Terve Suomi depression figures were retrieved. EHIS 2019 is about 7 years old, and wave 4 results were not found. The 2025 OECD Country Health Profile for Finland (published 11 Dec 2025) exists, but its mental-health figures were not extracted ([OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/country-health-profile-2025-country-notes_c0e8b8be-en/finland_311193cc-en.html)). Kela reports that the number of young disability pensioners fell in 2025 because the minimum age was raised; this is a **policy effect, not a health improvement** (headline only, [Kela](https://www.kela.fi/tietotarjotin/1452938-tyokyvyttomyyselaketta-saavien-nuorten-maara-vaheni-vuonna-2025-alaikarajan-nostamisen-vuoksi)). For pitching, "about 1 in 6 Europeans live with a mental health problem, and Finland is among the highest" is the most defensible headline. The **work-ability angle** (sickness allowance and disability pensions paid by Kela and pension insurers) is the strongest Finnish payer argument.

## Guidelines say "monitor", but nobody defines how after TMS

### The Finnish pathway runs from health centre to HUS and back

The Finnish Current Care Guideline (Käypä hoito "Depressio") uses **ICD-10 diagnosis: at least 2 core symptoms and at least 4 of 10 symptoms in total, for 2 weeks or more**. Treatment depends on severity. For suicide risk, the source lecture cites the C-SSRS scale. **The exact current version year is not confirmed.** A lecture by Prof. Erkki Isometsä (12 May 2026) cites a "2025" version, and the guideline's evidence-reference page is dated 8.1.2020 ([Isometsä lecture, [Secondary]](https://asiakas.kotisivukone.com/files/gpfinland.kotisivukone.com/tiedostot/Kevatkoulutus_2026/Isometsa_Masennus_ja_itsemurhavaara_120526.pdf); [Käypä hoito nix02769](https://www.kaypahoito.fi/nix02769)). The evidence-reference page shows that rTMS (including the THREE-D trial), ECT, esketamine and maintenance treatment all have evidence summaries in the guideline. The **2017 grade A for rTMS** in acute and treatment-resistant depression comes from the 2017 HALO review; whether that grade still stands in the current guideline was **not checked**.

| Severity | Monotherapy with sufficient evidence (Käypä hoito via lecture) |
|---|---|
| Mild | Internet therapies, psychotherapy |
| Moderate | Psychotherapy, antidepressants |
| Severe | Antidepressants, ECT |
| Psychotic | Antidepressant + antipsychotic, or ECT |

There are three phases: acute, **continuation (antidepressant always continued for 6 months after remission)**, and **maintenance for several years after a third episode**, or earlier after severe, psychotic or highly suicidal episodes. The guideline states that the patient's condition **must be monitored because of relapse and withdrawal risk**. For relapse prevention, **MBCT (8–16 sessions) and IPT (12–16 sessions) have grade-A evidence**. The lecture also cites a Finnish registry finding that suicide risk in the **first three days after psychiatric hospitalisation for depression was about 330 times the general-population rate**. This is a **statistic to verify** against the original study, and it concerns inpatients, whereas TMS is usually given to outpatients ([Isometsä lecture](https://asiakas.kotisivukone.com/files/gpfinland.kotisivukone.com/tiedostot/Kevatkoulutus_2026/Isometsa_Masennus_ja_itsemurhavaara_120526.pdf)).

The researchers found no data on who follows a TMS patient, or how often. The national mental health strategy (2020–2030), the role of occupational health and any adult "therapy guarantee" were **not researched**.

None of the guidelines retrieved defines a maintenance-TMS protocol, a remote-monitoring schedule or a digital relapse signature. Whether Käypä hoito recommends specific scales (PHQ-9, BDI, MADRS) is **not verified**. It is background knowledge only that Finnish primary care often uses BDI and PHQ-9 and specialist care uses MADRS. A home product would logically use **self-reported PHQ-9 (or QIDS-SR), with escalation to a clinician-rated MADRS**. This is an inference, not a guideline statement.

### Regulation: a "who needs a call" ranking is likely class IIa or higher

Under **EU MDR Rule 11**, software that gives information used for diagnostic or therapeutic decisions is **at least class IIa**. It becomes IIb if an error could cause serious deterioration, and III if it could cause death or irreversible deterioration. General wellness apps without medical claims are usually not medical devices. This is from a secondary explainer, not the regulation text, and the date of MDCG 2019-11 Rev.1 is reported inconsistently, so **[verify]** on the official MDCG site ([OpenRegulatory](https://openregulatory.com/mdcg/mdcg-2019-11)). The design consequence is an inference that needs regulatory review. A tool that only collects scores and shows them to clinicians may stay low-class or non-device. Once it **ranks or flags** patients, it is IIa at least, and **suicide-risk flagging could be argued up to IIb**. Interaction with the EU AI Act was **not researched**. Finland has **no permanent DiGA-style reimbursement**. A DigiFinland-coordinated reimbursement experiment (with Kela, Sitra and FinCCHTA) closed its call on 6 Aug 2025, and its outcome was not found ([Sitra 2025](https://www.sitra.fi/en/articles/use-of-digital-applications-in-healthcare-reimbursement-experiment-launched)). The realistic route to market is a FinCCHTA Digi-HTA assessment plus procurement by wellbeing services counties.

## Continuing what worked roughly halves relapse risk

### Relapse prevention: medication and MBCT are the proven anchors

The strongest relapse-prevention finding in depression is simple: **keep going with what worked**. Note that effect sizes from different meta-analyses are not directly comparable (odds ratios, hazard ratios, Hedges g, different control groups), so the table is not a ranking.

| Intervention | Effect | Certainty | Prevents relapse? | Home-deliverable? | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Continuing antidepressant after response | Relapse **about 18% vs 41%** on placebo (31 trials, 4,410 patients) | Not GRADE-rated; cite raw rates, as the summary's "70%" does not match them | **Yes** | Yes | [AAFP summary of Geddes 2003](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2003/0901/p952) |
| Maintain vs taper to placebo, primary care (ANTLER, n=478) | 52-week relapse **39% vs 56%**, HR 2.06, NNH 6 | Single large double-blind RCT; about 95% had 3 or more prior episodes | **Yes** | Yes | [ANTLER (UCL PDF)](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/10135848/1/nejmoa2106356.pdf) |
| Esketamine maintenance in TRD (SUSTAIN-1) | Stable remitters: relapse 26.7% vs 45.3% (NNT 6); stable responders 25.8% vs 57.6% (NNT 4) | Single phase 3 industry RCT | **Yes, in TRD** | **No**, given in clinic | [PMC6551577](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6551577/) |

The relapse risk factors in the Breedvelt analysis (**younger onset, shorter remission and higher residual symptoms**, HR 1.07 per point) match the NICE risk markers and the Miljevic predictors after TMS. Together they give a consistent, evidence-based list of inputs for a risk model. For psilocybin, a page sidebar dated 9 Nov 2026 says the US NDA submission was completed; this is **not independently verified**. A home product cannot give esketamine or psilocybin. It can support adherence, which matters because **stopping antidepressants doubled the relapse hazard in ANTLER**. It can also route people to MBCT, iCBT, exercise and light. The Finnish game-based DTx **Soihtu/Meliora** completed a 1,001-person RCT sponsored by Aalto, with sites including Aalto TMS in Espoo, but **no efficacy results were found**, so do not claim it works ([ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05426265](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05426265)).

## Implications for the product

The evidence points to one design: **a symptom-triggered rescue loop with a per-person sensing layer and a clear clinical action at the end**. The table links the evidence to the four judging criteria.

| Judging criterion | What the evidence says | Design implication | What not to claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Usefulness** | About 47 of 100 responders lose response by 6 months ([Senova](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:0f08a658-0949-4837-8cdf-a788794d9fd0/)); 84% of those with early worsening regained benefit with reintroduced TMS ([Janicak](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/durability-of-clinical-benefit-with-transcranial-magnetic-stimula/)); rescue maintenance at least as good as fixed schedules ([Akram](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:35db24da-5fca-413b-997a-be39e8c2c445/)); HUS has no booster protocol ([HUS](https://www.hus.fi/en/node/6912)); the Dutch-Flemish consensus calls for structured monitoring ([Dalhuisen](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:326d9d94-e467-445d-b968-4a7a6dc57803/)) | Replace "come back every 2–4 weeks" with "come back when the signal says so". ## Conclusion

The evidence changes the framing of the challenge. The question is not "what treatment can we deliver at home?". Home stimulation is approved in the US but contested, and it has never been tested after TMS. The question is **"how do we know, early and for this person, when to bring the clinic back?"**. Three verified findings support this framing: the size of early loss of response, the high success of TMS reintroduction, and the call from patients for structured monitoring. Two further findings protect the team from overclaiming: MONARCA II's null result and the internal-only validation of sensing models. The new idea is that the system learns per person which signal matters and which action helps. That idea is untested, so it is fair to pitch it as a hypothesis that the product would be built to test, not as a proven result.

The obvious next step after the hackathon is a **small prospective pilot with the HUS rTMS units (including Jorvi in Espoo)**. It would collect a personal baseline during the course and track PHQ-9 plus passive data for 6 months. The question to answer is whether personalised alerts arrive earlier than routine follow-up, and whether they lead to rescue sessions. A parallel Findata data request would size the population and show current relapse patterns in register data.
